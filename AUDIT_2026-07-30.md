## 1. Executive summary

**This is one of the healthiest small web games I have audited.** The engineering fundamentals — DOM-free rules core, single-source-of-truth balance module, deterministic weather/variance, save versioning with migration, a seeded headless simulator driving seven player models through the *real* hub, payload budgets enforced in CI, and content locks asserts by SHA-256 — are better than most commercial indie projects of this size. All 385 tests pass; lint, format and typecheck are clean; zero dependency vulnerabilities; the game is live and playable on GitHub Pages right now.

The weak spots are not where the prior audits said they'd be (those were largely fixed). Today's real problems are:

1. **A player-facing mechanic that does the opposite of its description.** Second Wind's `exhaustionResist` currently makes exhaustion arrive *earlier* and hit *harder*; the perk text says "exhaustion arrives later." Tests lock in the counterintuitive behavior and one test name is itself false.
2. **The truth gap moved, not closed.** The committed CHANGELOG's newest entry describes a rebalance (founding loop +18/−8 & +20/−14, greedy goal-rate ~23%) **that does not exist in the code** — the code still ships +15/−10, +12/−12 and a measured greedy goal rate of **100%**. This is exactly the doc/code balance drift the project's own postmortem (`notes/BALANCE_REGRESSION_POSTMORTEM.md`) swore off, one release cycle after that postmortem was written.
3. **A structural difficulty cliff that simulation makes visible but the docs don't own.** Engaged players literally cannot lose (100% goal rate at `concentrates` and above); disengaged players almost always lose (98%). There is no middle band where an attentive-but-imperfect human sweats — and real humans read free previews 100% of the time.

None of these require a rewrite. All three are cheap to fix. Details and the full findings register follow.

## 3. Balance state, measured

300 seeded runs, 61-day horizon, through the actual six-card hub (`poolMode: 'hub'`):

| Model | Death | Goal | Mean survival | End state (money/sanity/rep) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| random | 85% | 15% | 25d | 16 / 94 / 44 |
| doesnt_pay_attention | 98% | 2% | 18d | 18 / 78 / 29 |
| pays_attention_sometimes | 30% | 70% | 49d | 55 / 97 / 80 |
| average (calibrated 18% attention) | 53% | **47%** | 40d | 41 / 95 / 66 |
| greedy | 0% | **100%** | 61d | 56 / 99 / 97 |
| concentrates | 0% | **100%** | 61d | 97 / 99 / 100 |
| min_maxing | 0% | **100%** | 61d | 110 / 99 / 100 |

Three readings:

- **The calibration contract holds**: `average` at 47% is inside the committed 50% ±7% band. The README is honest that these are instruments, not people.
- **The curve is a cliff, not a slope.** Between "reads previews ~1/3 of the time" (70%) and "reads always" (100%) there is nothing left to struggle against. A real player reads the preview every time — it's free, one tap, and exact. The effective difficulty for an engaged human is ~0%, and the mastery layer (day 100) adds time, not resistance. The fantasy the README sells ("a week of pushing puts you in the ground") is one an engaged player never experiences.
- **The event pool drifts positive.** Weighted average of one fired event is **+2.08 sanity+money**. Rent escalation counters it, but the game quietly rewards you for existing; combined with exact previews, that is most of why attentive players can't die.

## 4. Findings (prioritized)

### P1 — player-facing or contract-breaking

**4.1 Second Wind's `exhaustionResist` does the opposite of its description.**
`docs/js/core/game-state.js:370-381` *adds* `exhaustionResist` to the exhaustion threshold: with Second Wind (`exhaustionResist: 8`), exhaustion starts at **33 energy instead of 25**, and at every energy below 25 the penalty is **equal or worse** than without the perk. Measured:

```
energy | no-perk | with Second Wind
   30  |    0    |    −1      ← "arrives later" actually arrives EARLIER
   15  |   −2    |    −3
   12  |   −3    |    −5
    5  |   −7    |    −8
    0  |  −10   |   −10
```

The perk's text promises "Resting recovers noticeably more, and exhaustion arrives later" (`docs/js/data/perks.js`). Tests `tests/balance.test.js:182` and `tests/systems.test.js:104` assert the current behavior, and the systems test is literally named *"widens the exhaustion threshold but also softens the fall"* — the softening half is mathematically false. `restBonus` makes the perk net-positive anyway, so players may never notice, but a game whose selling point is readable numbers should not sell a hidden malus as a bonus.
**Fix:** either flip the sign (`threshold − resist`, "arrives later") and re-tune the curve so mid-zone penalties stay ≤ base, or keep the mechanic and rewrite both the perk description and the two test names to tell the truth ("you tire sooner, but sleep repairs far more"). The first is probably the intended design.

**4.2 The committed CHANGELOG describes a balance that does not exist.**
`CHANGELOG.md` top entry (2026-07-30, committed in HEAD) claims: founding loop "+18 sanity / −8 money" and "+20 money / −14 sanity" (actual `docs/js/data/locations.js`: **+15/−10 and +12/−12**); "greedy 60-day goal rate ~23%, unachievable for >75% of greedy players" (measured: **100%**); and in the 07-29 section, "CI runs lint, formatting and typechecking before the coverage suite" (actual `.github/workflows/check.yml`: tests, asset check, coverage gate, balance report — **no lint/format/typecheck steps**). Either a rebalance branch was written up and never landed, or it was reverted without touching the changelog. Both are the precise process failure documented in `notes/BALANCE_REGRESSION_POSTMORTEM.md`.
**Fix:** decide which balance is canonical, align code and changelog to it, and treat "CHANGELOG numbers == simulation output" as part of the release contract the postmortem asks for. Consider a tiny CI step that regenerates the four headline sim numbers into the Actions summary on every PR (the `balance` job already prints them; the gap is that nobody reconciles prose against them).

**4.3 README's rare-event rate is off by ~2×.**
"Weights are 10 for Common and 2 for each Rare, so roughly one event in six is rare." Measured weighted rare share: **8.7% global (≈1 in 11.5)**, 5.4–13.8% per location (pools of 9–13 events each). One-in-six would need rare weight ≈ 4.3 at current pool composition.
**Fix:** cheapest truthful path is README copy ("roughly one in twelve"); if 1/6 is the intent, it's a balance change with test updates.

### P2 — real bugs, modest blast radius

**4.4 Reload-before-Continue rerolls the day.**
Autosave happens in the modal's `onContinue` (`docs/js/app.js`), not when the turn resolves. Production event RNG is unseeded (`new EventManager(createRng())` → `Math.random`), and `weatherSeed`/variance are deterministic but events are not. So: resolve a day, dislike the event, close the tab before Continue → reload replays the day with a fresh draw. The variance system's own comment promises a save "restores the same day, rather than re-rolling it in the player's favour" — that promise holds for weather/variance and leaks for events.
**Fix options:** (a) save immediately on resolve with a `phase: 'day_resolved'` marker and re-open the result modal on resume (true fix); (b) accept as a casual-friendly soft-retry and stop promising full determinism in docs; (c) seed the production RNG per-run from `weatherSeed` so a reload deterministically replays the same event (keeps current save timing, closes the exploit, still random across runs).

**4.5 Portrait lightbox leaks a keydown listener per open.**
`docs/js/ui/screens.js` → `openCharacterPopup()` removes an existing `.portrait-popup-backdrop` from the DOM but never invokes its cleanup, so each re-open adds another permanent `document` keydown listener. Cheap and harmless at small counts, wrong at session scale.
**Fix:** keep a module-level `currentClose` and call it before re-opening (one line), or attach one delegated document listener.

**4.6 `restart()` contains a dead, always-true branch.** `docs/js/app.js`: `else if (rng !== createRng)` compares an instance to the factory — always true, body is a comment. Delete the branch; the seeded/unseeded behavior it gestures at is already handled by `events.reset()`.

**4.7 First-event cadence differs between boot and restart.** `EventManager.initialize()` schedules from day 1 (first event lands day 3–6); `reset()` schedules from day 0 (day 2–5). After "Abandon run" the next run's first event can arrive a day earlier than a fresh boot's. Align both to one convention.

### P3 — consistency and hygiene

**4.8 Nudge/oversight inconsistency:** `getDailyNudge()` uses the base `EXHAUSTION_THRESHOLD` while `isExhausted` adds `exhaustionResist` — with Second Wind the energy bar warns at 26–32 but the daily cue stays silent.
**4.9 Almanac drift:** energy outlook hardcodes 25/75 instead of importing `EXHAUSTION_THRESHOLD`; the 4-day forecast applies *today's* season to all four days (`forecast(..., gs.getSeason(), 4)`), so it's wrong for the first days of March/June/September/December.
**4.10 Stale counts in living files:** `docs/js/data/locations.js` header says "twenty-two locations" (23 ship). Historical docs (DESIGN_REVIEW 22-loc/64-event, HANDOFF) are fine as history — but there are now **four audit files plus ASSESSMENT/HANDOFF/PROJECT_OVERVIEW** at root with overlapping scope. Keep `README`, `PROJECT_OVERVIEW`, `CHANGELOG`, one current audit; archive or delete the rest.
**4.11 `pendingAchievements` is reset on load** — an achievement earned on the day you reload never shows its toast. Cosmetic.
**4.12 CI doesn't run `npm run check`'s lint/format/typecheck legs** (§4.2). One-line fix: add `npm run lint && npm run format:check && npm run typecheck` to `check.yml`, or replace the steps with `npm run check`.
**4.13 Eager budget is 96.5% consumed** (3.86/4 MB). One more background (~100 KB) plus a couple of portraits (~30 KB each tier) will trip CI on an otherwise innocent content PR. Decide consciously: raise to 5 MB, or tighten per-image budgets in `optimize-assets.sh` and keep 4 MB.

### P4 — structural / repo-level

**4.14 Repository weight vs. payload.** Shipped game: 10.5 MB. Working tree source art: 243 MB. `.git`: **277 MB** with history squashed to two 2026-07-29 snapshots (so every master PNG exists twice in pack). `.gitattributes` declares `assets/** filter=lfs`, but the migration never happened — objects are raw blobs and `git lfs` isn't even usable in the current toolchain. There's also a stray 0.5 MB uploaded PNG in the first snapshot's root (deleted later, still in pack).
**Fix options:** `git lfs migrate import` over `assets/` (rewrites history — coordinate); or move masters to a separate `-art` repo / release artifacts and keep only a documented fetch step; or accept the weight and document `--depth 1` cloning. Status quo silently taxes every clone and CI checkout.

**4.15 Squashed history.** All pre-2026-07-29 development history (including the Godot era and the credited prior passes) is unreviewable. If that was intentional (fresh start), say so in README; if not, keep the old repo archived and linked.

**4.16 No real-browser or visual test layer.** jsdom covers logic and wiring, but nothing exercises WebKit/Blink rendering, touch, or visual regression; there is also a hard `matchMedia` dependency (fine in browsers, absent in jsdom — the DOM suites stub it). A minimal Playwright smoke (boot → play 3 days → resize to 360 px → screenshot) at PR time covers most mobile-risk for ~1 min of CI.

## 5. Long-term prospects

### The good news — this project is *durable*

- **Zero operating cost, zero runtime dependency.** Static files on GitHub Pages; no backend, no build, no framework to deprecate. This game can be maintained in an afternoon a month indefinitely and will still run in a decade as long as browsers parse ES modules. Very few indie projects can say that.
- **The hard infrastructure is already built.** Seeded simulation with player models, catalogue invariant tests, payload gates, save migration, asset pipelines — the things live-service-ish content games build *painfully late* are here *early*. Adding content is now a data edit plus one test run.
- **The design skeleton scales.** Location tags + weather + festivals + hosts + per-slot hub rotation give a lot of combinatorial content surface for very little code. The 60-day goal, perks, mastery ending and renovations form a clean arc: onboarding → pressure → mastery → cosmetic endgame.
- **It has a voice.** The writing is genuinely good — specific, warm, and funny in the right places ("Self" declining all follow-up questions; Brian's sign saying THE MIDDLE WAY IS NOT IN THE MIDDLE). In a market saturated with stat-manager skins, tone **is** the differentiator, and this game has one.

### The ceiling — content and difficulty, not code

1. **The difficulty question must be answered before any bigger release.** Today the tension curve is realized only for players who don't read. Options, in increasing invasiveness: (a) accept it — position the game as cozy-with-stakes ("death is for the distracted") and lean into narrative; (b) soften information — show exact numbers only sometimes (e.g. "How the day went" variance revealed only after committing at higher journey days),or make late-run hazards the engaged player's test (Kaden pressure events, winter spikes, reputation decay); (c) add an opt-in "Hard Winter" mode with harsher constants and no exact previews — one `balance.js` swap, sold as a feature. My recommendation: (c) — it converts the cliff into content without touching the calibrated default.
2. **Content cadence.** 235 events is deep for one 60-day run (a run sees ~20–30 of them), but a returning player exhausts favorite locations in 2–3 runs. The authored pipeline (events are 5-line data objects) is as cheap as content pipelines get; a sustainable rhythm is a "season" of ~20 events + 1 location + 1 festival per cycle, each with a test-run through `simulate`.
3. **The human-authored-asset goal is years of work at the current rate.** `CHARACTER_AND_LOCATION_TEMPLATES.md` targets fully custom art/profiles/events for 78 characters and 23 locations; today 1 of 78 portraits is custom. That's fine as a north star, but it should not gate releases. Keep the current gate (`notes/ART_STANDARD.md` — defect-driven replacement only) and treat provenance migration as background work.
4. **AI-provenance is a platform risk, not a code risk.** 77/78 portraits and all backgrounds are AI-generated. On GitHub Pages under MIT this is unproblematic; a future Steam/App Store/"featured on itch" push will face provenance questions from both platforms and players. Decide the story before a commercial step, and budget the commission queue accordingly.
5. **Cheap wins on the table, in order:** (1) wire lint/format/typecheck into CI; (2) fix the Second Wind inversion and CHANGELOG truth-gap; (3) close the reload-reroll window; (4) add a Playwright smoke; (5) PWA manifest + service worker (the game is already fully static — offline/installable is nearly free); (6) LFS-migrate or externalize source art; (7) localization infrastructure *only when a translator exists* — the data-driven event/location files make it tractable but 235 events is a real translation budget.
6. **Do NOT add:** a backend, accounts, telemetry in production, a framework, daily-reward retention loops, or monetization pressure. Every one of them would raise cost and erode the two actual moats: zero-ops durability and tonal trust. If telemetry is needed for balance, keep it dev-only as the roadmap already says.
7. **Community/share leverage.** The most underused asset is the deterministic seed itself: `weatherSeed` already makes a run a reproducible artifact. A "Share this seed / play this city" link (seed in URL, no server needed) would give word-of-mouth a concrete handle — roguelike daily-seed culture with an 11 KB code change.

### Bottom line

**Engineering: A−. Game-design calibration: B−. Documentation truthfulness: C+ (cheap to fix, embarrassing if not). Long-term viability: high.** The project has already survived its two likely killers — the Godot→web migration and the unloseable-game regression — and both postmortems produced durable guards. What remains is discipline: keep docs, code and simulation reconciled as one release contract; decide the engaged-player difficulty story; and protect the leanness that makes the whole thing maintainable. Do that, and secondbarnone has a realistic path from "impressive small web game" to a reference-grade example of how to build and operate one.
